# Claims
- Evolutionary optimization combines a candidate space, measured fitness, inherited variation and selection.
- Population memory and elitism protect observed incumbents, not diversity or generalization.
- Current fitness does not rank descendant potential; a lower-scoring parent can yield a better child under the same mutation budget.
- Retaining promising alternatives preserves direct starting points; benefit depends on variation, behavioral differences and evaluation cost.
- Exploration may replace a mechanism; exploitation may refine it.
# Decisions
- Title first; ordinary evolutionary algorithms precede the language-model operator.
- Population example compares two parents and their best tested children under equal hypothetical mutation budgets; explicitly separate from measured Max-Cut outcomes.
- Explain the reason for keeping alternatives before introducing the demo's cap-three deterministic ranking; no implied diversity mechanism.
- Question, two sequential mechanism blocks and answer per teaching frame; title is a single reveal.
