# 00a-circle-packing — teaching spine

## Claims

- Validity requires every circle to fit inside the square and every pair to avoid overlap; tangency is allowed.
- Each problem fixes N, allows variable radii and maximizes the sum of radii.
- Four circles of radius 0.15 sum to 0.60; four of radius 0.25 sum to 1.00 at the stated common centers.
- A packing program is the method; its returned arrangement supplies evaluation evidence.

## Decisions

- Put two problem frames immediately after the title, before any framework terminology.
- Move the former pre-demo problem definition here instead of repeating it later.
- Define center, radius, unit square, validity and score in plain language before using them.
- Retain the published 26- and 32-circle tasks and all numerical/model protocols.
- The small four-circle example illustrates the objective, not a demo outcome or optimality result.
- Plot exact coordinates: different radii illustrate the rules; matched centers with radii 0.15/0.25 show the score comparison.
- Preserve vector source and raster companions so browser frames and PDF exports show the same geometry.
