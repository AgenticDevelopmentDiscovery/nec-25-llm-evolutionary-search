# 05-diversity — teaching spine

## Claims

- An island is a separate evolving population with its own history; numerical seeds are evaluation trials.
- Migration balances independent exploration and sharing without guaranteeing global convergence.
- Exploration changes search mechanisms; exploitation refines useful structure.
- MAP-Elites preserves a strong representative per behavior bin; novelty measures behavioral difference.
- Quality and time can be Pareto-incomparable; scalarization requires an explicit exchange rate.
- Generation cost belongs to a search path; the classroom ranks quality and reports costs separately.

## Decisions

- Follow Chapter 10; preserve the shared Markdown, sequential reveals and Aggie Maroon design.
- Programs are evolved; circle arrangements are evaluated witnesses.
- Distinguish published problem formulations from our measured classroom results.
- Explain islands as a design extension; do not claim that three numerical seeds are three islands.
- MAP-Elites, novelty and Pareto retention are concepts, not implemented runtime policies.
- Use one illustrative cost trade-off instead of adding a lineage survey or another conceptual frame.

## Open questions

- Behavior descriptors and distance scales would need declared instrumentation before implementing novelty or bins.

## Not doing

- No claim of reproducing the unpublished AlphaEvolve engine, a record construction or global optimality.
